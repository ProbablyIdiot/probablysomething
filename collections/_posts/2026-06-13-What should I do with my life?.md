Thoughts on life, how I feel and the uncertainty of the future.

![A photo of a bike on a bridge, looking out over a dual carriageway towards a sunset](/assets/images/blogImgs/130626-bikeandsun.jpg)

I've been taking lots of bike rides recently. Not for commuting or getting to friends' houses, but just because I want to. I go out on a Thursday night, and just cycle around Worcester. I have Universty Radio York on my headphones, but other than that it's just me and my thoughts. This - combined with where I am in life - has led to lots of thinking about the future. 

Currently, I feel a strange mix of emotions. I'm excited for the future - I have so much to look forwards to (uni, girlfriends/getting married, having a family, having a career, having kids, etc), but at the same time, I'm terrified of losing what I currently have. This is mostly irrational - I have another year of my T-Level to look forwards to with all the same people around me - but not completely. Although I left my friends in the year below me a year ago, I still see them when doing work experience at my former school; now that they're also graduating, I won't anymore. **I won't see them every week anymore and that scares me.** And on top of all this, I dread the future. What will it look like? What if I make the wrong choice? How can I be sure that it won't all be downhill from here?

There's so much to decide- do I take a gap year? try and find a degree apprenticeship? or should I just go to uni and take the debt?

And I'm acutely aware of how disconnected my plans/hopes are from reality. Before starting college I told myself that this would be the year that I got a girlfriend. There would be so many new people at college (more than the 10 girls I've known since primary school) that *it would be impossible to not find a girlfriend.* However, fast-forward to the present and that is not that I live in. Because I took an IT course, **there are only two girls my floor of the college** and **none on my course specifically**. The culture at my college is that you don't talk to people not on your course - so college is a dead end. But I still have other options, right? I could find a girlfriend at parties. While they can be great friends they often want different things to me (sex before marriage, getting drunk, etc) - so near, yet so far. But there's still church! Except there are very few girls there who are within the very narrow age band for socially (and legally) acceptible dating at my age. There's one girl there who I like, but she doesn't feel the same. We're still good friends, but I think that's all it will ever be.

This brings me back to my main point - how can I decide which path to take, if I can't see where it will lead?

My current plan - the one I tell friends, family, and the random people who all seem to want to know what I'm doing for uni - is to do a degree apprenticeship. This is a great option. There's no debt, you get real experience, a wage and possibly a job offer. However, it does limit my options. Due to the cost of university in the UK, degree apprenticeships are a hot commodity. They're very competitive, so not very certain. But this isn't the main turn-off for me. I really want the "uni experience", my mum and dad try to disuade me from this "It's all just people getting drunk", but I think they're missing the other areas of it. I **really** want to do student radio, I want to join the societies and make friends and I want to go to parties with all my friends in one place ([this](https://smallcypress.bearblog.dev/lookiehere/)). While I can do some of these on a degree apprenticeship (you can still join societies), it won't be the same. Not being in halls or going to freshers' fair are two big ones. The sense of community and the physical nearness to other students just isn't the same when you spend most of your time off campus at "work". Similarly, the overlap between unis with student radio and apprenticeships at those unis isn't the biggest, so I would either need to really limit my options for an already competitive route, or give up on my dreams of uni radio.

On the other hand, I could just go to uni - I mean it solves all my problems right? Sense of community and lots of people to be friends with? Check. Easy choice of unis with student radio? Check. Ability to try new things with other similar people? Check. If only it were that simple though. The cost of university in the UK is **IMMENSE**. [The average debt leaving uni is £53,000](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/sn01079/), which is repaid with 9% of your earnings! This is bad enough, but taking uni might just be a worse choice (future job prospects wise) anyway. Unlike a degree apprenticeship, you don't leave with that real-world experience. As anyone who's been to school will tell you, however much they can teach in academia, it never truly prepares you for the real world. Finally, I might not enjoy the course as much. Uni is inherently written-work based. Therefore, there is much less hands-on, practical work (the stuff I enjoy), compared to the apprenticeship route. But I don't know if it's the biggest deal breaker.

Then there's the final, secret, third option. I could take a gap year(s). Joining [Operation Mobilization](https://www.uk.om.org/) or something similar would provide that sense of community and social aspect that uni would give me. Also, it would let me travel the world, experience new cultures and meet amazing people. I could (in theory) do this for one or two years, then settle down and do a degree apprenticeship. This is in theory the best of both worlds, but the threat of not getting on an apprenticeship still looms and I might not be able to do student radio.

Which leads me to my conclusion: Can I really put my future in jeopardy just so that I can have the oh-so-amazing uni experience for three years? Do I even have to? How much do I miss out on by doing a degree apprenticeship? And is student radio even that great?

In any normal story, this monlogue would end with a nice conclusion. Questions would be answered, and, even if not everything is revealed, the main character would know enough to leave the readers with a sense of optimism and hope for their future.

But this isn't a story.
