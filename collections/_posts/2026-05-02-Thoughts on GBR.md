I write this on the second of May 2026, roughly 5 months late to any news being discussed here (on time as always). Although this post may come off negative at points, I believe in GBR and renationalisation as a whole (although the current government overseeing the change fills me with nothing but dread), so keep that in mind.

Firstly, the plan as a whole seems quite sound (I'll get onto the ROSCOs later). Combining rails, stations and timetabling under one entity for the entire country is the logical course of action here, as it will vastly reduce overheads and wastage. Although British Rail had it's faults, by the end it was a very tightly integrated and efficient company, and I believe with enough funding it worked very well for it's purpose. While privatisation has seen a growth in passenger numbers, the higher ticket costs, constant delays and cancellations, ballooning government subsidies and other issues show that this system of privatisation simply doesn't work. Although it was supposedly designed with competition at it's core, the system incentivises over-promising and under-delivering, leaving customers with bad services. Therefore, the return of nationalised railways should hopefully lead to a better service for all who use the rail network.

However, although GBR is supposedly going to "[manage track and train](https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/rail-reform-and-great-british-railways)" in the UK, the new body **won't own the very trains it runs!** Despite the rolling stock companies being one of, if not the largest sources of corporate greed in the current system, **they will be kept around under GBR!** The government claims this is due to the companies investing in the trains, however these companies have continually rinsed TOCs, charging eye-watering sums for trains that have long since paid for themselves. I believe that although buying back current stock from these companies is off the table, GBR should at least consider buying it's own stock outright at point of ordering in the future.

![Rendered mockup of GBR's new livery on an IET](/assets/images/blogImgs/020526-GBRLivery.jpg)
Another aspect of GBR that seems to be very decisive is the new livery design unveiled by the DfT in December. Personally, I like the colour scheme and even the sharp, flag-inspired design. What I don't like, however, is the repetition of the design along the length of the train. Especially as practically all trains in the UK are now multiple units, I believe that the multi-coloured design should be exculsive to the ends of the train, and the intermediate carriages should be largely plain. A design similar to the Virign Trains Pendolino livery would work well here, with a striking design on the ends of the train that flows nicely through the middle. On the whole, however, I like the colour scheme and I'm excited to see it on a train when WMT's fleet gets repainted.

In conclusion, I am very excited by the possibility of renationalised railways, and althogh the current government seems hell-bent on reducing the country's quality of life, I am hopeful that GBR can deliver a much needed shake-up to the rail network.
