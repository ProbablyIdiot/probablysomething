**Lost Songs (Found) is an odd album.**
While it has a lot of the style of a classic TDCC album, it's not quite like Beacon or Tourist History. While the original opener to this review was going to be "An EP where most songs should've stayed lost", after re-listening it's not nearly as bad as I remember (admittedly, that was a very low bar, but still).

Many of the songs on this EP overuse electronic/synth sounds. Not In This Town for example, would be much improved with less of this, as in it's current state it isn't playlist-worthy. Tiptoe is just an all-round terrible song in my opinion. People who enjoy Pyramid might like this one, as to me they both seem quite similar to me. Finally, Too Much Coffee rounds out the EP on an odd note that I can't quite bring myself to enjoying.

However, there are some diamonds in the rough on this EP. 19 for example, is one of the best TDCC songs of all time. Even if the rest of this EP was filled with songs of Tiptoe quality, 19 would make it worth it. The song has thoughtful and simplistic lyrics and a catchy tune. Similarly, Hands Off My Cash Monty is a very good listen. Despite it's odd and completely unrelated name, the song expertly captures a feeling of being young, and the uncertainness of the future. Both these songs capture a different time for the band which I can relate to really well. That feeling of being free, but also unsure of the future is captured perfect, in a way I haven't seen by other artists.

Despite these very good songs, they aren't really what this EP is about. Something Good Can Work - Original Demo and Impatience is a Virtue are what this collection is **really** about. The whole EP is an insight into the band's song creation process, showing what songs are like before they receive that final polish that really makes them great. It's unusual for a band to release songs that they know are underbaked, but a really interesting prospect.
